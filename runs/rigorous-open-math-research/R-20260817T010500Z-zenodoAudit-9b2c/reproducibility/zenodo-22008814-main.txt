A Hardy-Gauge Contour Method for Density One of Zeta Zeros on
the Critical Line
Chaoyu Hu*
Jizheng Chen

Abstract
We construct a finite-dimensional Hermitian contour form from an analytic Hardy gauge
and a near-lattice packet family. Its zero-side residue representation converts off-critical-line
stationary geometry into negative inertia. On the arithmetic side, the same form admits an
exact right-edge decomposition whose principal term is the pullback of a completed lag operator
through the common packet/carrier analysis map. A direct Schur estimate gives a uniform spec-
tral lower bound for this lag operator on the mean-zero fibres. The stationary edge contribution
has rank o(NT ), while the regular remainder is o(NT ) in the relative Hilbert–Schmidt metric.
A finite-dimensional inertia argument then implies that all but o(NT ) zeros in a dyadic interval
lie on the critical line, and hence that N0(T)/N(T) →1.

Hardy’s theorem established infinitely many zeros on the critical line, and Selberg proved that
a positive proportion lie there [5, 58, 9]. The modern quantitative line begins with Levinson’s
mollifier method and its later refinements [8, 60, 3, 2, 4, 55, 56]. Closely related work on simple and
distinct zeros, pair correlation, and finite-dimensional positivity includes [42, 10, 51, 31, 18, 22, 11,
13, 40, 41, 1]. In particular, Alp¨oge and Furman obtained an unconditional proportion exceeding
two thirds for zeros that are both simple and on the critical line by combining a finite compression
of a Hermitian zero form with inertia.
A second strand concerns the size and statistics of ζ, its derivatives, and the Hardy Z-function.
Mean-value and discrete-moment methods were developed through the work of Gonek, Conrey–
Ghosh, Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek, Ng, Garunkˇstis–Steuding, Hughes, Milinovich, and later authors
[39, 28, 29, 24, 30, 38, 52, 43, 49, 50, 44, 45, 54, 7]. These results are especially close to the present
zero-side use of stationary points of the Hardy gauge and to the right-edge expansion through
Z′
1/Z1.
The arithmetic side also intersects the extensive literature on twisted moments, Dirichlet-
polynomial mean values, mollifiers, and ratios of L-functions [12, 36, 35, 14, 15, 16, 25, 26, 27,
34, 32, 33, 59, 6]. Adjacent work on zero gaps, derivative zeros, and critical-line geometry includes
[23, 17, 20, 19, 37, 21, 46, 47, 48, 53, 57]. The argument below uses a different assembly: the same
Hardy-gauge contour matrix is represented on the zero side and on the packetized right edge, its
principal lag operator is given a uniform spectral floor, and the remaining terms are separated into
low-rank and relative Hilbert–Schmidt classes.
The proof is organized around the normalized contour matrix introduced in Section 2, whose
zero-side and arithmetic-side representations are developed in the subsequent sections.
